## Casting

- Subtitles go to the TV too: the one shown when casting starts, or both when a second subtitle is on, the second above the first as in the player. A Chromecast always shows them; a DLNA TV shows them when it reads subtitle files named in the cast (Samsung, LG and mpv-based players such as Macast usually do).

## Playback

- A video no longer opens showing one corner enlarged for a few seconds: the last video's crop, kept for its picture size, is dropped before the next one opens.
- The first and second subtitle chosen last time come back on the next video again. When the video's details arrived before its file had loaded, the first subtitle was refused and the second one showed alone.
- When YouTube refuses the picture stream as a video opens, leaving sound and subtitles over black, the video is opened again with new addresses after 15 seconds.
- When a YouTube stream server answers 503 or not at all, Lumtub reads from the backup server YouTube names in the address, as YouTube's own player does, and keeps away from the failed one for 10 minutes. A live stream also asks again for about 10 seconds instead of switching between 1440p and 1080p, which came from the same server.
- Live streams above 1080p start about 20 seconds behind the newest moment instead of 6, as YouTube's own player does, so a broadcaster's upload pausing for a few seconds no longer stops the picture.

## Explore

- Searches translated for the explore language keep English phrases whole: "stand-up comedy live" in Italian is now "cabaret dal vivo" rather than a word-by-word "stare in piedi commedia vivere", so the live and other lists find videos again.
