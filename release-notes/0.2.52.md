## Changes

- iPhone and iPad: the picture no longer goes missing (or Lumtub closes) after switching back from another app: the player no longer resizes its picture while Lumtub is in the background, and a video decoder iOS took away meanwhile is made again on return. The log now notes when the app goes to the background and comes back.
- iPhone and iPad: swipe from the left edge to go back to the page before, as in other iOS apps; the page follows the finger.
- Two subtitles at once drawn by libmpv no longer run into each other: the second one now always sits just above the main one, whatever the fonts, the subtitle sizes, the main subtitle's line count, or the player's size (full screen, small window, mini player), on every platform. iPhone and iPad draw subtitles in PingFang, which they have, instead of a font they don't.
- Phones and tablets: in full screen, spread two fingers on the picture to crop it to fill the screen, pinch them together to see it whole again.
- Android: back on the first page sends Lumtub to the background, as Home does, instead of closing it, so what plays goes on (Android 11 and older closed the app).
- iPhone and iPad: names with invisible characters in them (a Korean channel's Hangul filler, a zero-width space) no longer show a box.
- iPhone and iPad with iOS 26.2 or later: VP9 and AV1 are decoded in hardware where the chip can, so 1440p and 4K play without the battery drain of software decoding. A TrollStore build (.tipa) also asks iOS for VP9 hardware decoding on older iOS.
- Where a device decodes neither VP9 nor AV1 in hardware, Lumtub says once why automatic quality stays at 1080p, and how to get more (in every interface language). On iPhone and iPad that is only a chip before the A12.
- Lighter hardware decoding: automatic quality and Low resource take the standard-range copy of an HDR video, as the picture is shown in standard range anyway; HDR cost a 10-bit decode (on some chips not in hardware) and tone mapping every frame. Quality first still takes HDR.
- Automatic quality stays within the size the hardware decoders take, as the start-up check measures it (a phone whose VP9 decoder stops at 1440p no longer gets 4K decoded on the CPU), and AV1 in hardware gives way to VP9 in hardware where AV1's decoder stops lower.
- Linux with NVIDIA's own driver: the start-up check now asks NVDEC, which libmpv uses there, so VP9 and AV1 decoded in hardware are recognised (they were treated as software, keeping automatic quality at 1080p H.264).
- iPhone and iPad: the playing video now shows on the Lock Screen, in Control Center and in the Dynamic Island, and other apps' music stops when a video starts (the sound was opened as mixable, which iOS keeps out of Now Playing).
- iPhone and iPad: a Mix with other apps switch (Sound settings) keeps other apps' sound playing with the video; iOS then leaves Lumtub off the Lock Screen and the Dynamic Island.
- iPhone and iPad: HDR videos decoded in hardware no longer show as a flat blue picture.
- Log level Off: nothing is written to the log at all (on Windows, no crash dumps either).
- Lighter software decoding: a picture above 1080p (on phones and tablets from 1080p) decoded on the CPU gets the cheap scaling at once; phones and tablets leave AV1's film grain to the renderer instead of the CPU; Low Power Mode, battery saver and a hot phone count as saving power; and Quality first picks VP9 over AV1 of the same size and frame rate where AV1 isn't decoded in hardware.
- Skin template: the newer fields (inactive title text, phone tab indicator, deck rim and colours, decoration seeds, sunken bevels) are written out.
- Preloading pauses after YouTube asks for a bot check or rate-limits, and skips videos that just failed.
- Stream pieces that are error pages or ignore the requested range are refused instead of being played.
- After switching Wi-Fi, mobile data or a VPN, playback reconnects at once instead of stalling for up to two minutes.
