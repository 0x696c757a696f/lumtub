## Learning pack

- **Find your level in two minutes.** A quick yes/no test: say which words you know, with a few made-up words mixed in to keep the score honest. Lumtub suggests your level and sets it for you. English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese and Russian; start it from the Learning page or Settings › Learning.
- **Difficulty on video cards.** Videos whose subtitles Lumtub has already read get a badge: Easy, Just right, Challenging or Too hard, from how many of their words you know. It asks YouTube for nothing extra.
- **Words come back when you meet them.** Look up a word you are learning while watching, and it returns in today's review. Its word card says how many videos you heard it in.
- **Portuguese.** A Portuguese dictionary, with Brazilian and European words told apart and read aloud in the subtitles' accent. Spanish words are marked as Latin American or from Spain the same way.
- **New Anki cards.** Words go to Anki as Lumtub cards, made for light mode, night mode and phones, with the line's sound, a picture from the video and a link back to its moment. The Anki button now saves an `.apkg` file: double-click it and Anki imports the deck. Importing again updates your cards instead of adding copies.
- In a video's difficulty, a Chinese or Japanese word made of characters you know counts as half known, and names in a sentence are not counted.

## Changes

- **Inter as the interface font.** Latin letters and numbers are clearer. Japanese, Korean and Traditional Chinese interfaces use their own character forms, and subtitles and word cards follow the language they are in. On Linux, a missing Japanese, Korean or Traditional Chinese font is downloaded when it is first needed.
